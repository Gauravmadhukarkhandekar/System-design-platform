# ACID vs BASE Properties

Two **names for consistency / availability trade-offs**: **ACID** (strong transactional guarantees) vs **BASE** (availability-first, **eventual** alignment). They are **not** “SQL vs NoSQL” laws — they describe **design goals** and **typical** distributed behavior.

For **A / C / I / D** spelled out with examples, see [Database Fundamentals](/learn/hld/database-fundamentals) (same ACID cards appear below for side-by-side reading).

## ACID properties

**ACID** keeps **transactional** updates **safe** under crashes and concurrency — the default story for relational OLTP engines (with **isolation level** nuances).

<AcidPropertiesCards />

## BASE properties

**BASE** is a **mnemonic** for systems that favor **staying available** and **scaling out**, accepting **temporary** divergence between copies of data.

<BasePropertiesCards />

<Callout variant="tip" title="BASE is not “no rules”">
You still define **conflict resolution**, **idempotency**, **versioning**, and **SLAs**. “Eventual” means **converge with a policy**, not “anything goes.”
</Callout>

## Key takeaways

<SummaryBox title="ACID vs BASE — recap">
- **ACID** = **reliable transactions**: atomic, consistent, isolated, durable — correctness under failure and concurrency.
- **BASE** = **basically available**, **soft state**, **eventually consistent** — favor uptime and scale; tolerate **temporary** divergence.
- **ACID** is harder to stretch to planet-scale writes; **BASE** trades **strong** global consistency for **availability** and partition tolerance (see **CAP** in practice, not as a binary theorem exam).
- **Polyglot** stacks: **ACID** for orders and money; **BASE-shaped** stores for feeds, cache, and derived views.
- Pick from **invariants and blast radius**, not slogans — **measure** and **document** what you actually guarantee.
</SummaryBox>
