# SLAs, SLOs & SLIs

The three pillars of **reliability engineering**: define what “good” means (**SLI**), set an internal target (**SLO**), and put a **customer-facing promise** with teeth (**SLA**). Use **tables** for reference math; use **boxes** for mental models and decisions.

## Understanding the hierarchy

<ReliabilityPillarBoxes />

<Callout variant="tip" title="Key insight: SLO should be stricter than SLA">
Keep **SLO stricter than SLA** (tighter availability / latency target internally than in the contract). The gap is your **buffer** before you pay credits, lose trust, or breach contract — and before on-call is fighting fires in public.
</Callout>

## SLI: Service Level Indicators

An **SLI** answers: *“How is the service behaving, in a way users feel?”* Pick a small set of **durable** signals you can alert and improve.

<MetricTable
  title="Common SLIs"
  subtitle="Formulas are illustrative — define “good” events precisely (e.g. which status codes count as success)."
  columns={['SLI', 'What it captures', 'Typical use']}
  compact
  rows={[
    ['Availability', 'Successful requests ÷ total requests (or uptime probes)', 'Almost every user-facing service'],
    ['Latency', 'Time from request to useful response (often p95 / p99)', 'APIs, gateways, checkout'],
    ['Throughput', 'Sustained work completed (RPS, jobs/sec)', 'Batch, streaming, workers'],
    ['Error rate', 'Failed or bad responses ÷ total', 'APIs, payments, writes'],
    ['Durability', 'Data retained correctly ÷ data entrusted', 'Blob, DB, backups'],
    ['Freshness', 'Age of data seen by users vs source of truth', 'Search indexes, caches, analytics'],
  ]}
/>

    </p>
    <ul className="list-inside list-disc space-y-2 text-sm text-rose-950 dark:text-rose-50">
      <li>Raw **CPU %** without user link</li>

<Callout variant="tip" title="Start small">
Aim for **3–5** SLIs that cover **availability**, **latency**, and **correctness/errors** before adding exotic metrics.
</Callout>

## SLO: Service Level Objectives

An **SLO** is a **target** for an SLI over a **window** (rolling 30 days, calendar month, quarter).

    <span className="rounded bg-violet-500/20 px-1.5 text-violet-900 dark:text-violet-100">99.9%</span>
    {' '}of requests have latency{' '}

    , measured over{' '}
    <span className="rounded bg-violet-500/20 px-1.5 text-violet-900 dark:text-violet-100">30 rolling days</span>.

    <li><strong className="text-foreground">Window</strong> — when you reset the story</li>

### The nines (availability targets)

<MetricTable
  title="Availability tiers (reference)"
  subtitle="Downtime budgets for a non–leap year; monthly ≈ 30 days for napkin math."
  columns={['Availability', 'Max downtime / year', 'Max downtime / month (≈)', 'Example context']}
  compact
  rows={[
    ['99% (two 9s)', '3.65 days', '~7.3 hours', 'Internal tools, best-effort'],
    ['99.9% (three 9s)', '8.76 hours', '~43.8 minutes', 'Many SaaS web apps'],
    ['99.95%', '4.38 hours', '~21.9 minutes', 'Stricter B2B, some clouds (varies)'],
    ['99.99% (four 9s)', '52.6 minutes', '~4.4 minutes', 'Payments, critical APIs'],
    ['99.999% (five 9s)', '5.26 minutes', '~26 seconds', 'Telco-grade, niche systems'],
  ]}
/>

<Callout variant="warning" title="Each nine is expensive">
Moving **99.9% → 99.99%** is not “10% better” — it’s roughly **10× less** allowed bad minutes. Expect **redundancy**, **testing**, **runbooks**, and **on-call** cost to jump.
</Callout>

## Key takeaways

<SummaryBox title="SLI / SLO / SLA — recap">
- **SLI** = credible **metric** of user-observable behavior.
- **SLO** = **internal** target on that metric over a **window** — drives error budget and engineering trade-offs.
- **SLA** = **external** commitment with **remedies** — keep it **easier to hit** than your SLO.
- **Error budgets** connect reliability to **velocity**: budget healthy → ship; budget burning → stabilize.
- More **nines** are **exponentially** costly — negotiate realistic targets.
- Keep the set of SLIs **small** and **owned**; revisit after major launches or incidents.
</SummaryBox>
