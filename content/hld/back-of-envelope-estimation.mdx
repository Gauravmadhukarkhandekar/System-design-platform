# Back-of-the-Envelope Estimation

## Introduction

**Back-of-the-envelope estimation** is doing rough calculations with simple numbers to size a system (storage, bandwidth, QPS) without a calculator. It is essential in system design interviews.

## Why It Matters

- Shows you can reason about scale.
- Helps validate whether a design can handle expected load.
- Uses round numbers (e.g. 1 million = 10^6) for quick math.

## Assumptions and Round Numbers

- **DAU (Daily Active Users)**: e.g. 100M, 10M, 1M.
- **Requests per user per day**: e.g. 10–100.
- **Seconds per day**: 24 × 3600 ≈ **86,400** ≈ **10^5** (for easy math).
- **Average size**: e.g. request 1 KB, response 10 KB.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Interview tip">
State your assumptions clearly. It is better to say "Assume 10M DAU" and then calculate than to guess silently.
</Callout>

## Example: QPS

- 10M DAU, 10 requests per user per day.
- Total requests per day = 10M × 10 = **100M**.
- QPS = 100M / 86,400 ≈ 100M / 100K ≈ **1,000 QPS** (peak can be 2–3x).

## Example: Storage (5 years)

- 100M users, 1 KB profile each = 100 GB.
- 10 photos per user, 1 MB each = 1 TB.
- Total ≈ **1.1 TB**; for 5 years with growth, maybe **5–10 TB**.

## Example: Bandwidth

- 1,000 QPS, 10 KB response = 10 MB/s ≈ **80 Mbps** outbound.

## Summary

<SummaryBox title="Key takeaways">
- Use **round numbers** and **clear assumptions** (DAU, requests/user, sizes).
- **QPS** = (DAU × requests per user) / 86,400; peak ≈ 2–3× average.
- **Storage** and **bandwidth** follow from data size and request rate.
</SummaryBox>
