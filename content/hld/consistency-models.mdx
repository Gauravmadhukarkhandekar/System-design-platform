# Consistency Models

## Introduction

In distributed systems, **consistency** defines what guarantees you get when reading data after writes. Different models trade off consistency for availability and performance.

## Strong Consistency

After a write completes, **every** read sees the latest value. No stale reads.

- **Pros**: Simple mental model; correct for critical data.
- **Cons**: Requires coordination; can limit availability and add latency.

## Eventual Consistency

After a write, reads **may** see old data for some time; eventually all replicas converge.

- **Pros**: High availability and performance; works well with replication and caching.
- **Cons**: Application must handle stale reads.

## Other Models

- **Causal consistency**: Reads respect cause-effect order.
- **Read-your-writes**: A user always sees their own writes.
- **Session consistency**: Same as read-your-writes within a session.

  W[Write] --> R1[Read]
  R1 --> R2[Strong or Eventual]

<Callout variant="warning" title="CAP">
Under network partition, you often choose between strong consistency and availability. Eventual consistency is common when you choose availability.
</Callout>

## Summary

<SummaryBox title="Key takeaways">
- **Strong consistency**: every read sees the latest write.
- **Eventual consistency**: reads may be stale; eventually converge.
- Causal, read-your-writes, and session consistency are useful relaxations.
</SummaryBox>
